If you ask a medical device team where the latest labeling details for a product are stored, you rarely get one clear answer. The artwork is in a shared folder, the specifications are in an Excel sheet someone emailed last week, and the final approval is buried in an inbox.

We built Uprevit to solve this exact problem.

## The problem with labels today

A medical device label looks simple: a name, a few symbols, a barcode, and some text. But every label has a lot of data behind it. Device class and markets. The standards you follow. The symbols you use and why. Dimensions, materials, storage conditions, and translations.

Most teams keep this data spread across tools that were never built for it:

- Spreadsheets for specifications, often with names like "specs final v2"
- Word or PDF files for label drafts
- Email threads for review and sign-off
- Shared drives for artwork and source files

Each tool works fine on its own. The trouble starts when everything has to match. A value changes in the spreadsheet but not on the label. A reviewer approves an old draft. An auditor asks who changed the storage temperature and when, and nobody can answer quickly.

None of this is anyone's fault. It is simply what happens when one product's labeling data is split across ten places.

## What Uprevit is

Uprevit is labeling management software for medical device teams. It gives every product one structured record and tracks every version, from first draft to final release.

Instead of hunting through folders, you open the product and everything is in one place, organized in seven tabs:

- **Product Information** for names, markets, Basic UDI, and device class
- **Compliance Information** for the standards and languages that apply
- **Label Components** for the physical labels, tags, and panels
- **Symbols & Graphics** for symbols, barcodes, and schematics
- **Product Specifications** and **Operational Parameters** for the numbers, in a clean table like Excel
- **Label Tags** for annotated label artwork

Products sit inside projects, and projects sit inside departments, so even with hundreds of products, everything stays easy to find.

## Versions you can trust

Every product moves through four simple steps: Draft, Submitted, In Review, and Released. Once a version is released, it is locked so nobody can edit it by mistake. If something needs to change, you start a new version. The old one stays in the history, so you can always see what was approved and when.

You can also compare versions side by side. The redline view shows what changed, so reviewers do not have to check every line manually.

## Reviews without the email chain

Approvals happen inside Uprevit too. You send one or more product versions into an approval workflow, assign approvers, and track progress in one place. Each approver can approve or request changes. If anything changes while a version is in review, approvers get a notice, so nobody signs off on something they have not seen.

When the workflow completes, the version is released and the earlier release is marked obsolete. No more guessing which draft is the final one.

## Getting data out

A record is only useful if you can share it. Uprevit lets you export a full product as a PDF, export specification tables, and create reports across all your products. That makes it easier to answer audit questions, send data to a printer, or check that a symbol is used the same way across a product line.

## Who it is for

Uprevit is built for the people who handle labeling at medical device companies:

- Labeling and regulatory affairs specialists who maintain label content
- Quality teams who need a clean history for audits
- Engineers and product managers who supply specification data
- Managers who want to see where every product stands

If you have ever rebuilt a label from three different files, Uprevit is built for you.

## What it is not

Uprevit is not a design tool, and it does not replace your artwork software. It does not make regulatory decisions either. It gives your team one reliable place to hold labeling data, review it, and show what was approved. Your team still makes all the regulatory decisions.
